🔐 Core Concepts

The CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is the foundation of cybersecurity. Every security strategy is designed to protect at least one of these principles. It represents the goal of network security. Cybersecurity aims to maintain...

  • Confidentiality — protecting information from unauthorized access
  • Integrity — ensuring information is accurate and intact
  • Availability — ensuring authorized users can access information

Source: idstrong.com

States of Data

Data exists in three different states, and each one of them must be protected differently.

📦 Data at Rest 🚀 Data in Transit ⚡ Data in Use
Typically files, databases, or other information kept on hard drives, USB drives, solid-state memory, DVDs, etc. Data being transferred between systems, like file sharing on a LAN or sending information over the internet. Data actively being created, accessed, processed, or loaded into an application or system memory.

👤 Authentication & Human Factors

Authentication

Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user or device. It is a critical component of cybersecurity, ensuring that only authorized individuals have access to sensitive information and systems.

There are several methods of authentication, including:

  • Password-based authentication
  • Two-factor authentication (2FA), which uses another method of authentication to increase security. (Ex: Google Two Step Verification)
  • Biometric authentication (e.g., fingerprint or facial recognition)

Password Best Practices

  • Use a mix of letters, numbers, and special characters/symbols
  • Use at least 8 characters in your password
  • Use BOTH lowercase AND uppercase letters
  • DO NOT use personal information, reuse passwords, or use dictionary words
  • Consider using a password manager to keep track of complex passwords

Human Factors

Human behavior is one of the biggest vulnerabilities in any system. Authentication can fail if users make poor and unsafe choices. The following table shows common human mistakes and how to prevent them:

⚠️ Common Human Factor Risks ✅ Improving Human Security
Weak or reused passwords that attackers could easily guess To prevent this, use strong password habits and password managers to create complex, unique passwords for each account
Falling for phishing emails designed to steal login credentials Instead, take phishing-awareness quizzes and simulations (like Google's phishing quiz) to practice spotting fake emails and links
Social engineering attacks (attackers trick users into revealing information) Constantly verify the identity of individuals who request sensitive information. Do not share information without validation
Unsafe habits, like writing down passwords or leaving devices unlocked To prevent this, store credentials safely and securely. It is strongly recommended to use a password manager
Human error, such as clicking malicious links or downloading unverified attachments Human error can never be completely eliminated, but it can be mitigated through Multi-factor authentication (MFA). MFA adds an extra security layer, even when something goes wrong

⚠️ Cyber Threats & Protection

Cyber Threats

Cyber threats target systems, networks, and individuals in many different ways. Understanding these threats, and how to protect against them, is essential for safe practices.

Social Engineering

Social engineering is attacks that trick or manipulate people who use technology. The goal of social engineering is to convince someone with higher level access to share information or provide access to an unauthorized person. Attacks rely on deception and manipulation to do so.

Humans are often the weakest link in security because they want to be helpful, avoid conflict, or sometimes they just make a mistake. Social engineering is used during the early stages of an attack.

Common techniques include:

  • Baiting
  • Shoulder Surfing
  • Piggybacking/Tailgating
  • Info written in workspace
  • Dumpster Diving
  • Pretexting - impersonation
  • Pretexting - wearing a uniform or using authority
  • Scareware (ex: phishing)

Phishing

Phishing is one of the most common cyber attack methods, used in most incidents. It's one of the top four biggest digital threats, and there's no code involved! It's all a lie.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is the use of fraudulent emails or fake websites to deceive someone into giving confidential information. A typical phishing attack involves tricking the victim into thinking that an email is from a trusted organization. The email contains a link that appears legit. When clicked, it takes you to a website that looks like something it isn't. They can steal all the information you enter.

A Few Special Types of Phishing Are...

  • Spear-phishing: targeting at specific individuals or organizations
  • Whaling: targeting high-level individuals
  • Smishing: phishing through text messages
  • Vishing: phishing through phone calls or voicemail

How to Spot a Phishing Email

  • Spelling errors or incorrect grammar
  • The sender's name and email address don't match
  • Hovering over a link shows a URL which doesn't match
  • Suspicious attachments
  • A strong sense of urgency
